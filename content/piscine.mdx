# The Piscine

The **Piscine** ("swimming pool" in French) is 42's selection process: about four weeks of full-time projects where you are thrown in the deep end and learn to swim. At the end, the school decides who joins the [Common Core](/wiki/common-core).

> [!NOTE]
>
> The format of the Piscine evolves over time and can differ between campuses. Treat this page as a general picture, and follow what your campus announces.

## How it works

- **Daily projects.** You work through a series of exercise modules, usually called `C 00`, `C 01` and so on, plus `Shell 00` and `Shell 01`. Each module is a set of exercises of increasing difficulty.
- **Weekly exams.** Exams take place in a locked-down environment, without internet or notes. You get exercises one at a time and must validate each before moving on.
- **Group projects.** Short projects done in a team with people you may not have met before.
- **Final exam.** The Piscine ends with a longer exam covering everything.

## What you learn

- The **Unix shell**: navigating the file system, permissions, basic commands, Git.
- The **C language**: variables, loops, functions, pointers, arrays, strings, memory allocation.
- **How to learn at 42**: reading `man` pages, testing your code, asking other pisciners, explaining your work.

## How you are evaluated

Each module you submit is checked twice:

- **By your peers.** Another pisciner reviews your code with you, asks you to explain it and checks it against the subject. You do the same for others. See [peer evaluation](/wiki/glossary#peer-evaluation).
- **By the Moulinette.** An automated grader runs tests on your code after the deadline. It is strict: a single error in an exercise usually fails it, and later exercises are not graded.

Your code must also respect **the Norm**, 42's coding style, which the [Norminette](/wiki/glossary#norminette) checks automatically.

## Tips for surviving the Piscine

- **Read the subject. Then read it again.** Most failures come from missing a detail: a file name, a forbidden function, a required prototype.
- **Test everything yourself.** Write your own `main` functions and try edge cases. Don't trust "it works on my example".
- **Talk to people.** Explaining a problem to someone often solves it. Helping others is the fastest way to learn.
- **Don't copy code.** Copied code is detected, punished, and leaves you unable to pass the exams.
- **Rest.** Sleep, eat and take breaks. The Piscine is a marathon, not a sprint.
- **Don't get stuck on one exercise forever.** It is fine to move on and come back later.

> [!TIP]
>
> Failing exercises is normal. Selection looks at your progress, your involvement and your exams, not only at how many exercises you validated.
